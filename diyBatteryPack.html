🔋 DIY Altoids Battery Pack

A simple sixth-grade electronics project that repurposed a 12V car USB charger circuit into a homemade portable USB power source inside an Altoids tin.
Early Electronics
Power Conversion
Soldering
Repurposed Hardware

Overview

This was one of my earliest hands-on electronics projects. I built a small portable battery pack using an Altoids tin as the enclosure, a 9V battery as the power source, and the circuit board from a disassembled 12V car auxiliary USB charger.

The idea was to take something that normally plugs into a car outlet, reuse its USB charging circuit, and package it into a small portable container. It was a simple project, but it introduced me to the idea that electronics could be taken apart, understood, modified, and rebuilt into something new.

How I Built It

  • Started with an empty Altoids tin as the enclosure.
  • Disassembled a 12V car auxiliary USB charger and removed the internal circuit board.
  • Connected a 9V battery to the charger circuit board as the power input.
  • Positioned the USB output so it could be accessed from the tin.
  • Packaged the wiring, battery, and board inside the Altoids tin.

Technical Breakdown

The reused car charger circuit acted as a basic power conversion module. In a car, that circuit normally steps down the vehicle’s 12V supply to a USB-friendly output. In this project, I connected it to a 9V battery instead, using the same circuit to create a small portable USB power source.

While the project was simple, it helped me understand the relationship between a power source, a circuit board, an output connector, and an enclosure. It was also an early lesson in voltage conversion, packaging, and practical wiring.

What I Learned

  • How to safely take apart a consumer electronic device and identify useful components.
  • How batteries, wiring, and circuit boards connect together in a real physical system.
  • How enclosure design matters, even for a simple electronics project.
  • That small, improvised projects can be a strong starting point for learning engineering.

Reflection

Looking back, this project was basic, but it was important because it made electronics feel approachable. It was one of the first times I realized I could modify existing hardware and turn it into something functional. That curiosity eventually grew into more advanced projects involving control systems, embedded systems, hardware design, and power electronics.
